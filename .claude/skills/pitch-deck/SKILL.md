# Pitch Deck

## Intent

Turn finished product and market deliverables into a persuasive, one-glance pitch deck. This skill enforces a proven narrative arc and the sourcing discipline that compression threatens — it *assembles* the existing work, it doesn't re-analyze. It composes with the strategy and discovery skills (reused, not re-derived) and consults [[ux-usability]] and [[accessibility]] for slide legibility.

## Rules

1. **Synthesis, not new thinking.** Build every slide from finished deliverables; each slide traces to a source ([[audience-segmentation]], [[market-analysis]], [[competitive-analysis]], [[positioning]], [[mvp-scoping]], [[product-specs]]). If the source doesn't exist yet, stop and send it back to that skill — don't invent it on a slide.
2. **Sourcing integrity under compression — the headline rule.** A deck's short lines are where invented precision and fake quotes creep in. Figures stay **ranges with the firm named**; quotation marks go **only** around text verbatim from a named source; our synthesis is stated plainly as ours; never manufacture a single clean number (e.g. a TAM) the sources don't give.
3. **One message per slide.** The title states the takeaway; the body proves it. Aim for ~10 slides (Kawasaki's density discipline) — if a slide carries two messages, split it.
4. **Problem at magnitude, led by the pain.** Name the specific gap *and* show its scale. Open on the human pain or the value, not on the mechanism or the framework — or the product reads as a "vitamin," not a painkiller.
5. **Show the mechanism, don't assert the adjective.** For any trust/quality/impact claim ("responsible," "safe," "better"), show *how* — the concrete mechanism and a measurable outcome — never a free-floating adjective.
6. **Make the core idea a designed centerpiece.** The one "underlying magic" gets a real visual, and the deck's diagrams share one consistent visual language.
7. **Solution maps to the problem.** Show the steps between pain and resolution, slide-for-slide against the problem — not a feature list.
8. **A product pitch ends on vision, not an ask.** Keep the narrative arc but drop the fundraise tail — close on the vision and the one thing to validate next. Keep an explicit "ask" only when it genuinely is a fundraise.

## Checklist before calling it done

- [ ] Every slide traces to a finished source deliverable (nothing invented)
- [ ] Figures are ranges with the firm named; no single invented number
- [ ] Quotes only around verbatim source text; our synthesis stated as ours
- [ ] One message per slide; title = the takeaway
- [ ] Problem shown at magnitude and led by the pain, not the mechanism
- [ ] Trust/quality claims shown as mechanisms + outcomes, not adjectives
- [ ] Core idea is the designed centerpiece; diagrams share one visual language
- [ ] Closes on vision + what to validate next (an "ask" only if it's a real raise)

## When unsure

If a number can't be traced to a source deliverable as a range, leave it off the slide. If a slide needs two takeaways, it's two slides. If a claim can't be shown as a mechanism, it's an adjective — cut it or find the mechanism. If the audience is investors writing a check, keep the ask; otherwise end on vision. Hand the positioning statement to [[positioning]], the sizing to [[market-analysis]], and the PRD to [[product-specs]] — this skill arranges their output, it doesn't reproduce it.

## Based on

- Guy Kawasaki — the 10/20/30 rule (≤~10 slides, one message each, a readable floor = density discipline).
- Sequoia Capital — the pitch narrative arc (purpose → problem → why now → solution → market → product → differentiation → close).
- Rockefeller Foundation — *Digital Storytelling for Social Impact* (pair every story with a measurable outcome).
- The project's own sourcing-integrity norm (ranges with the firm named; quotes only verbatim; synthesis stated as ours).
- Composes with [[positioning]], [[market-analysis]], [[competitive-analysis]], [[audience-segmentation]], [[mvp-scoping]], [[product-specs]], [[ux-usability]], [[accessibility]].

## Examples

**Sourcing under compression — ❌ invented precision vs ✅ range with firm named**
- ❌ "The AI-governance market is $2.6B, growing 40%."
- ✅ "AI governance: ~$2.6–5.8B by 2030–34, CAGR ~35–45% (The Business Research Company; P&S Intelligence; IMARC)."

**The close — ❌ fundraise tail vs ✅ product-pitch vision**
- ❌ "We're raising $500K to hire a team and go to market."
- ✅ "Next: validate the first climb with real nonprofits — do they seed a policy, then trust and reuse a governed Skill?"

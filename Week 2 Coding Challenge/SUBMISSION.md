## What I Did

It's been a lot of work and really challenging for me which makes sense as it's basically developing the vision of the product, scope, and learning how to set up technical requirements and architecture. I worked very closely with Claude, and tried my best to check all the parts of the work, but I am still not sure if all of it makes sense. I will definitely be coming back, refining, and iterating as I am building it.

My process was two-fold.

- First, I followed all available Tech Fleet templates when working through Audience Segmentation, Market Analysis (market research, competitive analysis, feature analysis, and positioning), Vision & Scope, Epics & Features, and creating Kanban board. I did those in Figma and recorded in Notion as well. I also started moving things on the Kanban board to intake and creating requirements.
- For the rest of the specifications (build plan, non functional requirements, technical requirements, user requirements) I have queried NotebookLM to give me a roadmap and worked closely with Claude to create these specifications.

I have also created skills from every deliverable and improved my skill governance structure, evaluation process, and how skills are triggered and routed. My skills library spans now 17 skills across six categories, and I am trying to improve and keep it organized. I have used NotebookLM to query the sources and extracting the best practices that were discussed in the course, and auditing my set up based on that. From technical standpoint, the main improvement was that now Claude selects skills by category (for now I mostly have skills in the product category, but will be adding as residency progresses), while testing, accessibility, UX, and documentation skills are always checked and applied to every task because those are designated as quality guardrails. In addition, I started ADRs to keep track of the decisions I have been making, which I think will improve both learning and execution.

## What I Learned

I have learned lots this week when it comes to product work and truly seeing the full cycle of it and doing it with Claude. I learned how to move from backlog to requirements, and how to ground my concept and create the roadmap for the development, make it concrete from vision to workable tasks.

Next time, I will give more context to Claude and maybe distill more things in my head so I can evaluate his proposals and outputs with more confidence. I will also work on being more efficient as I spend way to much time doing it, and I want to be able to do it faster and trust myself more. The lessons from this exercise will help me in project team work in the future in many ways. By becoming more confident with both 1) co-creating with AI and 2) becoming knowledgable project and product manager will allow me to contribute, support my teammates better, and ship products that are scalable and solving real problems in the world.
